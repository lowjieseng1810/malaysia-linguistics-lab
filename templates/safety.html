Safety · Respect · Responsibility

Learn with respect. Explore with care.

This project introduces selected Malaysian languages as living forms of communication connected with real people, communities, histories, and places. Learning should never turn a community into a curiosity or reduce a living culture to a collection of objects.

The goal is not to speak for communities. The goal is to create a careful learning space that makes sources, limitations, and the need for community review visible.
PRINCIPLE 01

Respect living communities

The languages presented here belong to living communities. They should not be treated as costumes, decorations, historical relics, or entertainment. Cultural material is presented to support understanding of the people and places connected with each language.

PRINCIPLE 02

Cultural sensitivity

Not every tradition, object, ceremony, story, or form of knowledge should automatically be placed online. Material that may be sacred, restricted, private, or sensitive requires greater care and should not be presented simply because it is available somewhere on the internet.

PRINCIPLE 03

Source transparency

Photographs, maps, videos, and research materials should be connected to their sources wherever possible. Historical artworks should be identified as historical representations, and images should not be presented as evidence of present-day life when they are not.

PRINCIPLE 04

Language diversity matters

A language name can include regional and local differences. This is especially important for language traditions such as Bidayuh. Learning material should identify the variety being taught instead of presenting every community as if it speaks in exactly the same way.

PRINCIPLE 05

Learning content has limits

A digital learning resource cannot replace fluent speakers, community knowledge, or full linguistic documentation. Material under development should remain clearly marked as under review until it has been checked against reliable sources and, where possible, reviewed with appropriate speakers or community contributors.

Under Review ≠ Community Verified
PRINCIPLE 06

Corrections are part of the project

Language and cultural information can be incomplete, outdated, or wrong. Corrections should be welcomed, reviewed carefully, and supported with context or reliable evidence. Community knowledge should not be dismissed simply because a digital resource is already published.

PROJECT POSITION

An independent educational project

Malaysian Language Heritage Explorer is an independent educational project. It does not claim to officially represent the Iban, Kadazan-Dusun, Bidayuh, Mah Meri, or any other community featured in the project. Unless clearly stated otherwise, content should not be interpreted as official community endorsement or as a replacement for community-led language education.

A responsible language project should be able to show what it knows, what it does not yet know, and who still needs to be heard. This page will continue to develop alongside the project, especially as learning materials, community review, source records, and correction systems become more complete.
